1. SECTOR OVERVIEW

The European freight and logistics market was valued at approximately 1.31
trillion euros in 2025, growing 4.2% year on year in nominal terms and roughly
1.1% after adjusting for inflation. Growth has decelerated from the
post-pandemic peak of 2022, when nominal growth exceeded 11%.

Road freight remains dominant, accounting for 73% of tonne-kilometres moved
within the European Union. Rail accounts for 17%, inland waterways 6%, and
other modes the remainder. The modal share of rail has been broadly static for
a decade despite policy intent to shift freight off roads.

Fragmentation remains the defining structural feature. The ten largest
operators account for under 20% of total market revenue, and the long tail
includes an estimated 550,000 firms operating fewer than ten vehicles.


2. MARGIN STRUCTURE

Operating margins across the sector typically fall between 3% and 9%.
Specialised segments earn more: temperature-controlled and pharmaceutical
logistics regularly exceed 10%, while general road haulage frequently operates
below 4%.

The margin differential reflects barriers to entry rather than pricing power.
Temperature-controlled freight requires validated equipment, continuous
monitoring, and regulatory qualification that a general haulier cannot acquire
quickly. Air charter operations similarly benefit from limited capacity and
the time-critical nature of the freight.

Contract logistics and warehousing occupy a middle position, with margins
sensitive to utilisation. Industry practice treats sustained utilisation below
85% as a sign of structural overcapacity rather than cyclical softness.


3. LABOUR

Driver shortage is the sector's most frequently cited operational constraint.
Industry bodies estimated a shortfall of approximately 425,000 drivers across
Europe in 2025. The average age of a European heavy goods vehicle driver is
47, and fewer than 6% are under 25.

Driver attrition rates between 15% and 30% are common. Operators reporting
attrition below 15% are typically those that have completed a structural pay
review rather than those competing on non-monetary benefits alone.

Pay restructuring has become the standard response, though its cost is
material: a 10% increase in driver base pay typically consumes between 2% and
4% of a road-focused operator's revenue.


4. DECARBONISATION

Electrification of light commercial fleets has progressed considerably faster
than heavy vehicles. Sector-wide, electric vehicles represented roughly 9% of
light commercial fleets in 2025, against under 2% of heavy tractor units.

The disparity is infrastructural rather than technological. Long-haul corridors
lack charging capacity at the power levels heavy vehicles require, and the
investment required is generally beyond what individual operators will commit
to unilaterally. Several large operators have consequently declined to publish
heavy fleet electrification targets, while others have published targets
contingent on public infrastructure investment.

Hydrogen remains under evaluation. No major European operator has committed to
hydrogen at scale, and most published roadmaps treat it as a post-2035
consideration.


5. REGULATION

Proposed revisions to European Union driver hours rules are the most
significant pending regulatory change. Draft proposals would reduce maximum
permitted driving periods and extend mandatory rest.

Sector analysts estimate the aggregate impact at between 0.4% and 0.9% of
operating profit across affected operators, though the effect varies widely
with network design. Operators with dense short-haul networks are less exposed
than those dependent on long-haul corridors.

The legislation has not been finalised, and its scope has changed materially
between drafts. Operators have generally declined to quantify the impact
precisely, citing that uncertainty.


6. CONSOLIDATION

Merger and acquisition activity totalled approximately 18.4 billion euros
across 214 disclosed transactions in 2025, down from 23.1 billion euros in
2024. The decline reflects financing costs rather than reduced strategic
interest.

Integration performance is poor by the sector's own accounting. Studies of
disclosed transactions suggest the median integration runs 40% longer than the
acquirer's original public estimate, with system consolidation the most common
source of delay. Legacy data quality is repeatedly identified as the specific
failure point, particularly where acquired entities operated separate
transport management systems.

Leverage discipline has tightened. Net debt to EBITDA above 3.5 times is now
commonly treated by lenders as a constraint on further acquisition, where 4.0
times was tolerated in prior cycles.


7. OUTLOOK

Sector forecasts for 2026 cluster around 3% to 5% nominal revenue growth, with
margin recovery dependent on whether capacity discipline holds. Consensus
expects continued divergence: specialised segments growing faster and earning
more than general haulage.

Analysts caution that sector-level averages conceal wide dispersion. Individual
operator performance in this market has historically correlated more strongly
with segment mix and network design than with sector conditions.
